Contract review

Redline

Checks a contract against your playbook, flags each clause as aligned, a deviation or one for a lawyer, and suggests the redline.

A concept demo with fictional contracts. Everything in it is scripted; nothing is sent to a model.

The problem

Most contracts a legal team sees are routine, like NDAs, but each one still has to be read against the team’s playbook: how long confidentiality may last, which law applies, whether a residuals clause is acceptable. It is slow, repetitive work, and the risky clauses hide among the standard ones.

How it works

Redline splits a contract into clauses, matches each one to a rule in the playbook and sorts it into one of three piles, so the lawyer starts with what matters.

Contract→Clause splitter→Playbook rules→Findings→Lawyer review
  1. The contract is split into numbered clauses, each kept with its exact wording.
  2. Each clause is matched to a playbook rule, such as “confidentiality lasts at most three years”.
  3. It is marked aligned, a deviation, or for a lawyer to review, with the reason and the playbook rule beside it.
  4. For deviations, Redline suggests replacement wording taken from the playbook’s fallback positions.
  5. A reliability tab runs the reviewer on clauses a lawyer has already labelled and shows where the two disagree.

Design decisions

  • Every finding points to its clause. The exact wording that triggered a flag sits next to the rule it was checked against, so a lawyer can verify it in seconds.
  • Three outcomes, not two. When a clause doesn’t clearly pass or fail, it goes to a lawyer instead of being forced into a yes or no.
  • The playbook is the source of truth. Positions and fallbacks are written by the legal team and can be edited; the tool applies them, it doesn’t invent them.
  • Measure before trusting. The reliability tab compares the tool’s answers with a lawyer’s on known clauses, and shows every disagreement, not just a score.

How I’d build it

The same way I built my other tools: one step at a time. First the clause splitter, then each playbook rule as its own check, each tested on labelled examples before joining them into one review screen with the reliability tab.

About this demo

The NDA, the parties, the playbook and the test results are all made up. The reliability numbers are illustrative, from a small fictional test set; they show what the tab would report, not a measured result.
