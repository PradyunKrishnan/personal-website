Clause 9.2 · Residuals · Deviation

In the contract

Nothing in this Agreement restricts the Recipient from using information retained in the unaided memory of its personnel, including ideas and know-how.

Suggested redline

Nothing in this Agreement restricts the Recipient from using information retained in memory. Nothing in this Agreement grants the Recipient any right to use Confidential Information, whether or not it is retained in memory.

Playbook rule

No residuals clause. Confidential information stays protected even if someone remembers it.

Why it was flagged

This clause lets the other side use anything its staff remember, which empties the confidentiality duty.

How does this work?

Redline reads a contract against the team’s playbook and sorts every clause into three piles, so the lawyer starts with what matters. The animation is scripted, but the reliability check is real: simple playbook rules run in your browser, with no AI model.

Contract→Clause splitter→Playbook rules→Findings→Lawyer review
  1. The contract is split into numbered clauses, each kept with its exact wording.
  2. Each clause is matched to a playbook rule and marked aligned, a deviation, or for a lawyer.
  3. Deviations get suggested wording from the playbook’s fallback positions.
  4. The reliability tab really runs the rule checker on 24 clauses a lawyer has labelled, and shows every case where the two disagree.
